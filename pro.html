10 · Kanban & health

      
Your to-do list, next to the code.

      
Free gives every project a task list and a “where I left off” note. Pro turns the list into a kanban board, and a finished task can be linked to the commit that did it. A health dashboard checks all projects at once — README, .gitignore, uncommitted work, forgotten projects, files that look like passwords or API keys — with one-click fixes, and the gallery greets you with the project you were last working on.

12 · Releases & sharing

      
Finished? Put it out there.

      
Anyone can export a clean ZIP (no build folders, no .git) and a 1200×630 share card. With Pro, one dialog bumps the version, builds Release, packs the program with its DLLs and data and publishes a GitHub release with the .exe attached. Share cards come in square and story sizes and more styles, ready for Instagram, LinkedIn or your Discord.

      

    

  







  

    

      
13 · Sync & your own actions

      
Same gallery at home and at school.

      
Point sync at a OneDrive, Dropbox or Google Drive folder and your settings, theme, profile, collections, favourites and custom actions follow you to your other computers. Descriptions, notes and tasks live in a small file inside each project, so they travel with your code in Git.

      
Custom actions are your own buttons: “Open in PowerShell”, “Run tests”, “Deploy”… Free has two; Pro has as many as you like, globally or per project, in the ⋯ menu and in Ctrl+K.

14 · Automatic backups

      
Backups you don't have to think about.

      
Pick a folder — a second disk or OneDrive — and the gallery zips every project that changed since the last backup, in the background. Build folders, node_modules and .git are left out (unless you want the full history), old copies are rotated, and a backup restores into a new folder so it never overwrites your work. The free disk cleaner sits right next to it.

16 · Compare & changelog

      
The changelog writes itself.

      
Pick two versions — a tag or any commit — and see the commits between them, the changed files and a Markdown changelog grouped into new features, bug fixes and the rest. Copy it, or use it as the release notes for your next GitHub release in one click.
